Three approaches to media measurement & targeting

Metered panels, virtual panels, and simulation answer the same business questions with very different data contracts. This deck contrasts the three on data flow, cost, latency, and the trust story you can tell about each.

The same questions, three answers

Who reaches what. Who buys what. Where to target. Which creative wins. Each methodology is a different bet on where to spend the data budget.

Axes of difference

  • Inputs — recruited panel, fused census-scale data, or seed + brief.
  • Latency — months, weeks, or hours to first result.
  • Granularity — panel size, fusion resolution, or the seed's identity table.
  • Falsifiability — what can you point at and say "this is wrong"?

Industry tends to ask: how much do I trust the answer, and how fast can I get it? The three methodologies sit at different points on that frontier.

Strengths

  • Real human behaviour, longitudinal.
  • Audited, currency-grade outputs.
  • Transparent provenance — every row is a real meter read.

Limits

  • Slow to scale; recruitment is the bottleneck.
  • Expensive; panel cost grows linearly with size.
  • Coverage gaps in long-tail content and channels.
  • Granularity bounded by panel size and stratification.

Strengths

  • Census-scale coverage from existing feeds.
  • Single source of truth across screens (TV + digital).
  • Reuses TAM/DAM investments rather than replacing them.

Limits

  • Heavy ETL; fusion quality bounds output quality.
  • Needs all source feeds present and aligned.
  • Fusion artefacts can hide where coverage is thin.
  • Per-device-class re-runs (PC, smartphone, tablet).

3. Simulation (AI-first)

emerging

Data flow

seed                  ─►  graph DB (Neo4j)
  panel CSVs              one node per panelist
  brief / stimulus        edges = propensities         engagement
  rules                                       ─►       runner    ─►  posts.jsonl
                                                       (OASIS-              decisions.jsonl
                                                        shaped)             metrics.json
                                                                            report.md

  no recruited panel.   no fusion.   the seed is the contract.

Strengths

  • Hours to first result; cheap to re-run.
  • Explainable per-agent — every decision has a trace.
  • Can model scenarios that have not happened yet.
  • Reproducible — same seed, same world.

Limits

  • It is a prediction, not a measurement.
  • Output quality bounded by the seed's behavioural fidelity.
  • No ground truth on its own. Needs a peg.
  • LLM drift; non-deterministic without locked seeds + caches.

The trust problem with simulation

"Simulated reach" alone is unfalsifiable. Anyone asked to bet budget on it will reasonably ask: compared to what?

The peg pattern

For every simulated metric, emit one corresponding measurable metric in the same run. The measurable becomes the reliability indicator — its real-world value can be looked up, and the gap (or correlation over time) is the trust signal. 

  simulated metric              measurable peg                 reliability indicator
  ─────────────────             ──────────────────             ────────────────────────
  reach                    ─►   downloads / internet traffic   ratio sim/measured = R
  engagement (likes etc.)  ─►   shares, comments, time-on      delta over rolling window
  appreciation index       ─►   star ratings, NPS, returns     RMSE vs reported AI
  purchase intent          ─►   cart adds, coupon redemption   conversion-rate ratio
  reply / discussion       ─►   forum post counts, search      lift vs baseline

The peg does not need to be perfect. It needs to be independently observable so the simulation can be scored against something the audience already trusts.

What this buys us

Positioning

  • Not "replace the panel" — triage what is worth a panel.
  • Not "replace fusion" — extend where fusion has thin coverage.
  • Not "ground truth" — hypothesis pruning with a reliability indicator.

Operating model

  • Run twenty cheap simulations.
  • Commission one expensive panel for what survives.
  • Track the sim-vs-peg ratio over time; promote sims as the ratio stabilises.

A worked example

A streamer wants to predict reach for a Sunday 21:00 drama launch. Run the simulation against a 10-panelist seed; emit simulated_reach alongside predicted_iplayer_starts. The day after launch, fetch real iPlayer starts from the existing reporting pipeline. The ratio is the reliability indicator for this genre.

When to use which

NeedPickWhy
Currency-grade audit / contractual reporting 1. Metered panel Only directly measured data clears the bar.
Cross-screen single source of truth at scale 2. Virtual panel Fusion is the only path to census-scale per-person.
Fast hypothesis triage, "what if" scenarios, creative testing 3. Simulation Hours not weeks; cheap to re-run; explainable.
Long-tail content / niche audiences 2 + 3 Fusion thins out; simulation extends with seeded personas.
Predicting a launch that has not happened 3 (with peg) Only simulation can answer pre-launch; the peg builds trust.

These are not exclusive. The mature operating model uses all three — each answers a different question.

Where this leaves us

What's true today

  • Metered panels remain the audit currency.
  • Fusion is the production answer for cross-screen scale.
  • Simulation is fast, cheap, and explainable — but unfalsifiable on its own.

What we propose

  • Adopt the peg pattern: every simulated metric ships with a measurable counterpart.
  • Track sim-vs-measured ratios over time; promote sims to decision input as ratios stabilise.
  • Use simulation for triage and scenarios; keep panel + fusion for currency and scale.

Open questions

  • Which peg metrics are routinely available, by client and vertical?
  • What ratio variance is acceptable to call a sim "trusted" for a given decision class?
  • How do we govern seed updates so simulations stay current without losing reproducibility?
